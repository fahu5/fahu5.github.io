About

I'm Fahmida, a recent CS graduate from Varendra University, Bangladesh (CGPA 3.86/4.00, full scholarship). I like building AI that actually gets used — not just benchmarked.

My main project has been HemoCount, a lightweight YOLO model that automates cell counting from hemocytometer images, currently being written up for journal submission. I've also worked on more data-driven projects: a published study linking parent-daughter relationship quality to depression in adolescent girls in Bangladesh, and a book chapter on AI for waste management and circular economy.

Before research took over, I spent a lot of time on student leadership and competitions — Campus Director for Hult Prize at my university, and researcher on the Team Voyagers group that won World Champion honors at NASA Space Apps 2023. That mix of building things, presenting them, and working with people is still how I like to work.

I'm now looking for a PhD where I can go deeper into AI — whether that's biomedical applications, environmental problems, or the broader social impact of AI on communities like mine. I'm not narrowly locked into one domain; what pulls me in is AI that's actually useful and fair to the people it touches.

Research Interests

I am interested in applying AI, ML across a few areas — biomedical image analysis, environmental problems, and the social impact of AI more broadly. What connects them is building models that work well even with limited data or limited computing power, since that's the reality in a lot of places, including where I'm from.

Concretely, that's meant:

  • Medical image analysis — my HemoCount project used YOLO to automate hemocytometer cell counting, and I want to push further into other diagnostic imaging tasks.
  • Health informatics — my published work used survey data to study depressive symptoms in adolescent girls in Bangladesh.
  • AI for circular economy — a book chapter on using AI for waste management and circular economy systems.

What ties these together is wanting AI that's lightweight and deployable, not just accurate in a paper. That's the kind of research I want to keep doing in a PhD.
